FD-1 · Demo

Try the model.

FD-1 is a decision model behind an API. Give it a text and a decision to make — a yes/no, a choice between options, a judgement call — and it answers with three things: a decision, a confidence, and an escalation flag on the calls it should not make alone. The examples below are genuine pre-computed FD-1 outputs — tap one and read the verdict the way the API returns it.

Clear calls — the model is sure
Borderline — below the line, escalated to the larger model
Confident — and wrong

Sarcasm still fools it. Reviewers spot-check confident calls too — every correction becomes training data for the next version. These are 10 hand-picked examples — the measured rates are in §02 below.

escalation threshold 0.49 — tuned once for a 15% budget, then fixed
How to read a verdict. Decision is the call itself — positive, negative, or neutral in these examples. Confidence is the model’s self-reported certainty — 0.99 means “I’m sure”, 0.41 means “I’m guessing”. It is not a calibrated probability: well-calibrated on SMS-style inputs (ECE ≈ 0.05), less reliable on phishing-style inputs (ECE ≈ 0.21), and overconfident on sarcastic or adversarial text. Escalation: calls below the 0.49 line are escalated — the larger model re-checks them, and its answer becomes training data. That’s the loop.

02 — Measured

What the lab measured.

On entailment (MNLI), FD-1 is right 92.0% of the time, and its confidence there is close to honest — expected calibration error 0.057. On the harder multiple-choice panels it is right less often: 0.543 on AG News topics, 0.530 on BoolQ yes/no, 0.241 on MMLU. Its confidence is also less reliable there — calibration error reaches 0.516 on HellaSwag.
