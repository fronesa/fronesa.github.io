05 — Benchmark by benchmark

Every benchmark, side by side.

FD-1’s figures do not sit alone. Below, each of the seven benchmarks gets its own small table — one gets a graph — and every row in it is a decision model: FD-1, the decision APIs it competes with (Jev, Perplexity’s Decider, Microsoft’s Decision-1, OpenAI’s Decisions API), a purpose-built classification model where one was measured on the same run, and independent measurements of those same products where they exist. Each figure carries who measured it and under what protocol. Where a competitor publishes nothing for a benchmark, the table says so; silence is data, and it is never filled in by inference.

Nothing here is averaged across benchmarks, and nothing is ranked across different task forms. The protocol column is what tells one measurement from another.

How to read these tables

FD-1’s row is first in every table, and the exact variant it ran is stated above it — task form, split, sample size, scoring rule, seed. Every other row is a decision model or a decision API: we compare decision models with decision models; general-purpose LLMs are a different category and are not charted here. Where a figure comes from an independent measurement rather than the vendor, the row says so. ECE appears only where a real figure was published with its method, and ECEs are never ranked — no two published ECEs in these tables share a dataset, a binning, and a confidence definition at the same time. (Perplexity’s one published calibration figure — Decider v1.1, ECE 0.0159 — was measured on the vendor’s own 1,497-row development set, not a held-out public benchmark, so it has no row in the tables.) Where a competitor publishes nothing, the table says “not published” and explains why. If a genuinely shared protocol ever exists — same items, same harness, every product — we will publish that comparison and let it say what it says.
